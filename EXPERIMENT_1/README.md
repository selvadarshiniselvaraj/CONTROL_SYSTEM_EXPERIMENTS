<h3 align="center">Transfer Function of an Armature-Controlled DC Motor</h3>

---

## Aim

To obtain the transfer function of an armature-controlled DC shunt motor.

## Apparatus Required

- Armature-controlled DC motor setup
- DC power supply
- Voltmeter
- Ammeter
- Tachometer
- Connecting wires
- Patch cords

## Theory

The DC motor is basically a torque transducer that converts electrical energy into mechanical energy. DC servo motors have high torque-to-inertia ratio, compact size, and approximately linear input-output characteristics, allowing them to respond rapidly and be controlled accurately in closed-loop control systems.
In an armature-controlled DC motor, the field current is kept constant and the speed of the motor is controlled by varying the voltage applied to the armature. There are two 
circuits called the field circuit and armature circuit. Let if and ia be the field and armature current 
respectively. The torque T generated by the motor is given by

$$
T(t)=K i_a(t) i_f(t)
$$

Where k is a constant. The generated torque is used to drive a load through a shaft. There are basically two ways of controlling the DC motor. If the field current is kept constant 
and if the input voltage is applied to the armature circuit, then the motor is called an “armature controlled” 
DC motor. If the armature current is kept constant and the input voltage is applied to the field circuit, the 
motor is called a “field controlled” DC motor. If the field current if is kept constant and 
the input voltage is applied to the armature circuit then the motor is an armature controlled dc motor. If if 
is constant equation (1) can be written as, 

$$
T(t)=K i_a(t) 
$$

The armature circuit equation is:

$$
V_a(t) = R_a i_a(t) + L_a\frac{di_a(t)}{dt} + E_b(t)
$$

where:

- $V_a$ = Armature voltage
- $R_a$ = Armature resistance
- $L_a$ = Armature inductance
- $i_a$ = Armature current
- $E_b$ = Back EMF

The back EMF is proportional to the angular velocity:

$$
E_b(t) = K_b\omega(t)
$$

The torque developed by the motor is proportional to the armature current:

$$
T_m(t) = K_t i_a(t)
$$

The mechanical equation of the motor is:

$$
T(t)=J\frac{d^2\theta}{dt^2}+f\frac{d\theta}{dt}
$$

where:

- $J$ = Moment of inertia
- $f$ = Viscous friction coefficient
- $\theta$ = Angular displacement
- $K_t$ = Torque constant
- $K_b$ = Back EMF constant

By combining the electrical and mechanical equations, the transfer function of the armature-controlled DC motor can be obtained.

## Procedure

1. Connect the armature-controlled DC motor setup as per the circuit diagram.
2. Keep the field supply constant.
3. Apply the required armature voltage to the motor.
4. Measure the corresponding armature current and motor speed.
5. Note down the required motor parameters and readings.
6. Write the electrical and mechanical equations of the motor.
7. Apply the Laplace transform to the equations.
8. Combine the equations to obtain the transfer function.
9. Record the calculated transfer function.
